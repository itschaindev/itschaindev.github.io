## Capitalism

How would one define capitalism?

`An economic system in which the means of production and distribution are privately or corporately owned and development occurs through the accumulation and reinvestment of profits gained in a free market.`

To understand the working of the modern economy, I enrolled myself into an economics masters and took 3 courses in the first semester. In all of them, one thing that was common was that no economic theory can be applied as is to the market. There are simultaneously more than one economic theory at play in real world market. This means, it is difficult to analyse how the market works, let alone predict it. All one can do is, create a boundary to limit the number of variables to a level that a human brain or even a machine can comprehend. Reason why I think it is a mistake is because we are so deeply connected in this globalised world that setting a boundary means assuming that the variables that have been left out will not affect the outcome of the analysis. Useful, but not accurate. 

When the US started to beacon the successes of initial era of capitalism, there were a different scale of economies at play compared to what we have in this age. To begin with, there was no internet! The scale was limited to the speed of the information flow. And information flow was slow, very very slow, relatively. This limited the growth of the companies upto a certain level. And the initial principles, I myself was in favor of i.e. a free market to anyone who has the courage to, were something that resonated with common people. Irrespective of anyone's background, you now had a chance to become what you can dream of. You were limited by your own thoughts. The society has set you free. And for that era, this was huge!

But, there is a fundamental flaw in the current idea of capitalism. Constant growth! Stagnancy is unacceptable.

Whenever you think of the past and the urge comes to point fingers, one need to consider the circumstances at that time. You cannot judge something from the past with modern standards (unless it is inherently immoral, a rabbit hole I don't want to go into). Probably at that time, very few people were thinking or even aware of what climate change is and its implications. At least, not as well talked social cause as it is today.

Constant growth, for businesses that require natural sources, is a dead end. Considering the lengths one would need to go to in order to grow it. And I do agree that there are businesses that can grow YoY. But even those can lead to exploitation of human resources if not natural resources. And if we look historically, after a business becomes a conglomerate, they dominate that field, leaving no room for competition or acquire competition. Leaving the power to consolidate within a few hands. This was not what was intended by the initial principles of capitalism. Free market, a chance to become anything. One can argue it is still possible and to a degree sure, but try making another YouTube like platform and you will realise what I'm talking about.

What's that level where a business should not be allowed to grow more? There are some regulations pertaining to that called anti-trust law suits, but are limited in extent and heavily lobbied. Barely having any meaning.

## Sustainability

So when these companies talk about their sustainable business model, it all feels like a fucking joke to me. And their carbon off-setting points just wanna make me puke. Most of the companies talking about off-setting their carbon emissions, are investing into non-profits who try their best to accomplish or in true sense, reverse what these companies have done to the environment. Makes no sense. There is no way to track if all the investments are going to the intended places and are actually off-setting the emissions of the companies. Like if an airline promises they are funding a non-profit that platns trees, how would one calculate if the tree has actually been grown to the size till it really offsets the footprint? Tracking of such things is definitely an immensely complicated process. I tried doing that once and it just isn't practically fast enough solution that we need right now.
